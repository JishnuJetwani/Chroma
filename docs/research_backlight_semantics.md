# Scene-aware hints for backlight, clipped skies, and white clothing

Research date: 2026-10-21. Scope: local scene diagnostics, segmentation, and integration with the current recipe renderer. This memo used local image inspection and read-only code inspection; it did not upload the photograph, install dependencies, download model weights, or launch training. Proposed experiments below have not been run.

## Recommendation

Add explicit clipping/detail diagnostics first, then test frozen semantic masks as **confidence-weighted hints and limits on adjustments**. The strongest immediate candidates are MediaPipe SelfieMulticlass for clothing/skin/person and SegFormer-B0 ADE20K for sky/vegetation/person. Apple Vision is a useful native Mac alternative for the person mask. Do not interpret “person against a bright sky” as a command to brighten the entire person. In this image, most of the white shirt already has substantial luminance; protecting its folds is more useful than uniformly lifting it.

The existing renderer is a suitable foundation. Its one-preview analysis, normalized-coordinate maps, and whole-image recipe shared by all tiles should remain intact. A semantic helper should run once on the source preview and be cached. Semantic confidence should reduce an optional correction when uncertain, rather than create a sharp edited/unedited cutout.

## What the photograph actually contains

The supplied local JPEG is 640 × 360. Visual inspection shows a person turned away from the camera, a bright white shirt with visible folds, extensive almost-white sky, a warm bright area beside the head, a pink/white flare over the shirt, and low-contrast vegetation. There is no visible frontal face to use as the primary exposure reference.

## Current implementation and the missing information

- [inference.py](../src/photograder/inference.py) loads an oriented sRGB original, makes a ≤1024 px preview, calls `model.analyze`, and caches an `EditRecipe`. Sliders and export reuse it. This is the natural location for one-time semantic inference and diagnostics.
- [models.py](../src/photograder/models.py) has a 256 px encoder and a decoder conditioned on features, style, and one luminance guide. Its usual adjustment maps have a 64 px longest side. Optional global context adds eight RGB/luminance mean/std values; it does not add class-specific statistics, clipping counts, or a recovery confidence map.
- [models.py](../src/photograder/models.py) optionally gates highlight/shadow specialist heads by luminance alone. A bright shirt and a bright sky can therefore receive similar gating despite requiring different treatment.
- [renderer.py](../src/photograder/renderer.py) multiplies linear RGB by a ratio derived from exposure and log-luminance detail contrast. This preserves RGB ratios before clipping. It does not subtract an additive veil or independently restore local chroma. Detail contrast can accentuate folds and vegetation, but is not equivalent to flare removal.
- The renderer computes low-resolution guided coefficients once, samples maps in global coordinates, and includes a `2 * radius` halo for the local operator. [Existing tests](../tests/test_renderer.py) already cover tiled/whole-image equivalence and intermediate-highlight preservation.
- [metrics.py](../src/photograder/metrics.py) measures any-channel clipping and new clipping. Extend the diagnostic vocabulary with all-channel saturation and near-white plateaus; do not replace the existing metric or call every bright pixel an exposure fault.

## Diagnostics that do not need another network

The following are proposed features, not calibrated physical measurements:

1. **Information availability:** any-channel saturation, all-channel saturation, a soft near-white score over roughly 250–255/255, local gradient/detail energy, and plateau size. Calculate exact clipping on native decoded pixels before resizing; downsampling can hide single-pixel/channel clipping. Distinguish one clipped channel with remaining structure from a constant RGB-white patch.
2. **Region-relative exposure:** log-linear-luminance percentiles and bright/dark fractions within person, clothing, sky, and non-sky background. Exclude clipped pixels and uncertain boundaries when estimating a region's statistics. Empty masks need an explicit validity bit. A sky/person luminance difference is a backlight cue, not a desired correction amount: white clothes can legitimately be brighter than the landscape.
3. **Contrast and color cues:** local log-luminance variance or inter-percentile spread at two scales, local minimum RGB (“dark-channel” statistic), and RGB chroma spread. Low contrast plus lifted minima plus proximity to a strong light source supports a *possible veil* cue. White fabric and smooth sky are counterexamples, so this cue needs semantic protection and an uncertainty output.
4. **Geometry and uncertainty:** top-connected bright regions can serve as a conservative sky-candidate baseline; it will fail on ceilings, walls, snow, and bright objects touching the frame. Bright-region adjacency to the subject and a broad surrounding glow support backlight detection. Do not infer camera position or true depth from these heuristics. Softmax entropy/margin, mask agreement under horizontal flip or small resize, and disagreement between person helpers can reduce confidence; these are quality proxies, not calibrated probabilities of correctness.

The dark-channel paper itself excludes sky from its basic prior and documents failures for objects whose color resembles atmospheric light, including white objects. Its atmospheric model also has limits around sunlight/sky effects. Therefore use its statistic as a hint, not a global inverse-haze prescription for this white-shirt photograph. [Original dark-channel paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/cvpr09.pdf).

Lens flare is a separate mechanism involving reflections/scattering inside the camera. Existing flare datasets explicitly separate light sources, glare, reflective flare, and streaks; prominent published datasets focus on nighttime. That supports a separate flare-confidence concept, but does not establish that a nighttime removal model will fix this daytime image. [Light-source-guided flare research](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Qiao_Light_Source_Guided_Single-Image_Flare_Removal_From_Unpaired_Data_ICCV_2021_paper.html), [Flare7K paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06570).

## Three bounded experiments

### 1. Diagnostic hints without retraining

Implement a local analysis report and overlays containing clipping/near-white maps, multiscale contrast, and manually verified shirt/sky samples. Compute new summaries both globally and excluding likely sky. Record predicted exposure/contrast distributions in the same regions. Compare the current checkpoint unchanged against a conservative recipe limiter that only suppresses *additional* local lifting where highlight headroom is exhausted and suppresses local contrast on fully clipped flat interiors.

Use the input to the local stage for headroom calculations, because the global LUT may already alter brightness. Store the source clipping mask separately because a global darkening does not restore lost information. Smooth all limits. A per-pixel headroom limit can be based on `log2(target_max / max(linear_RGB))`, but the actual combined exposure-and-detail gain must be checked, not exposure alone. For the initial test, choose conservative limits on validation images and report them explicitly rather than treat them as learned truth.

This experiment should establish whether losses are driven by a missing diagnostic or by renderer/model capacity. It also produces a small evaluation set for the semantic experiments. There is no reason to add a VLM just to count clipped pixels or measure local contrast.

### 2. Frozen semantics + bounded existing recipe

Run MediaPipe multiclass once for `P_person`, `P_clothes`, and skin; evaluate a separate SegFormer-B0 run for `P_sky` and vegetation. First inspect masks on the supplied image and a small varied local set. Include native Apple accurate person segmentation as a Mac-specific comparison if helper setup is straightforward. Make simple manual masks for 10–20 diagnostic images to measure the difference between segmentation error and editing-policy error.

Cache masks at 256–512 px in normalized source coordinates, with source checksum and model/revision/processor identifiers. At recipe construction, use the masks to bound exposure and contrast, not set fixed target brightness per semantic class. Protect bright low-chroma clothing from extra exposure; permit modest contrast where folds remain. In confident foliage, try a small positive local contrast delta, with a noise/texture confidence bound. Preserve the clipped sky's flat interior and make any change there broad and smooth.

Compare unchanged recipe, photometric-only limits, automatic semantic limits, and manual-mask limits. The automatic/manual difference quantifies how much further segmentation work is worthwhile. Limit the first policy to existing exposure/contrast controls: if washed-out chroma remains, record the limitation instead of claiming the sky/person masks solve it.

### 3. Train a small hint adapter, then test one extra renderer capability

Freeze the semantic helper. Add 6–8 maps such as person, sky, clothes, clipping, available detail, possible veil, semantic confidence, and validity to the decoder. Widen the decoder's first fusion convolution and copy its original input weights; initialize new input-channel weights to zero so the old checkpoint is the exact starting behavior. Add masked region moments to the global conditioner only as a separate ablation. Initially freeze the LUT bank and most encoder weights so changes can be attributed to the hints.

Train with the same scene splits and paired reference supervision as the baseline, plus shirt/highlight preservation and uncertain-boundary penalties. Predict hints from the actual training input, including corruptions; using perfect clean-target masks only during training would hide the backlit segmentation failures encountered at inference. Mask dropout and uncertainty perturbations should teach a fallback to the original model when hints are absent or unreliable.

If this improves localization but leaves the veil, add **one** bounded extra operator as a separate arm: a low-frequency RGB affine correction or additive veil field, close to identity, with strong clothing/sky protection and gamut penalties. The existing bilateral-grid path provides an internal architectural control. A subtractive veil can restore apparent contrast where multiplication cannot, but subtraction in white/flat/noisy areas can produce gray clothing, hue shifts, and negative channels. Keep its correction small and evaluate it independently; semantics are not evidence that a physical atmospheric inverse is valid.

## Acceptance tests and failure probes

- **Seams:** reuse the same semantic maps and prepared coefficients for every tile. Compare whole-image and tiled output at several odd image sizes and tile sizes, with mask boundaries deliberately crossing tile boundaries. Begin with the existing `7e-5` FP32 comparison tolerance and inspect maximum error specifically on tile borders. Any new local filter must include its full support in the halo; adding masks alone does not justify per-tile inference.
- **Halos:** use a dark silhouette against white sky, white fabric against both dark and equally bright backgrounds, fine hair, and narrow gaps. Compare guided vs bilinear map upsampling. Test equal-luminance different-color boundaries because the current scalar luminance guide cannot distinguish them. Measure overshoot/undershoot and new gradient extrema in a 3–10 px boundary band; visually inspect at 100%. A guided filter can align to existing image edges, but cannot recreate a semantic boundary erased by flare. [Fast guided-filter method](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00996).
- **Cloth detail/color:** annotate stable shirt interiors away from flare. Measure newly clipped pixels, bright/dark fold gradient preservation, luminance percentiles, and Lab/chroma drift. A white shirt should retain its neutral appearance and fold ordering. Include colored and black shirts so the protection does not become “all clothes must be white.”
- **False positives and abstention:** include high-key portraits, snow, clouds, white walls, haze-free backlight, warm golden-hour photos, overcast skies, dark skin, people seen from behind, multiple small people, and no-person images. Zero-confidence or missing hints must reproduce the baseline recipe exactly. Perturbed masks must fade toward the baseline, not create a visible cutout.
- **Clipping honesty:** constant clipped patches must not acquire invented texture. Report fully clipped and near-clipped fractions separately from any-channel clipping. Do not reward turning a white plateau gray as “highlight recovery.” For this photo, judge better vegetation, shirt detail, and restrained color while accepting the sky's missing information.
- **Performance and portability:** benchmark first/warm analyze time, helper load time, peak resident memory, MPS/CPU parity where applicable, and preview/export agreement after resizing to common dimensions. Pin helper assets and hash them; repeat inference offline after downloading the public model assets. Cache invalidation must include orientation, image checksum, processor config, and helper revision.

The best initial deliverable is a measurable diagnosis and a conservative semantic-gating comparison. A pretrained segmenter supplies useful scene labels; it does not supply the correct photographic treatment, recover clipped content, or guarantee reliable masks in this particular backlight.
